PROOFWORK
The complete development roadmap for our EPITECH Job Board project
Bro, we're going to build something much more interesting than a conventional job board.
I've considered the ideas we discussed, the weaknesses of each approach, and the requirements in both EPITECH documents.
My proposed direction is PROOFWORK: an evidence-first job platform where people demonstrate their abilities instead of relying entirely on a résumé.
We're not going to build LinkedIn with a different color scheme. We're going to rethink one part of the recruitment process, then build an entire working product around it.
Our mission

Help early-career applicants discover opportunities, understand what employers need, demonstrate relevant skills through real projects, and submit stronger applications with evidence attached.
1. What exactly are we building?
PROOFWORK: Show your work. Find your place.




















11









Visual inspiration for the product's direction, not final designs.We're building a full-stack recruitment platform with three connected experiences:

The Opportunity Board
Users discover jobs and internships, filter opportunities, open detailed listings, and submit applications. This satisfies EPITECH's central job board requirement.



The Proof Portfolio
Applicants link projects, repositories, demos, certifications, and other evidence to specific skills. They can choose which evidence to attach to each application.



The Opportunity Intelligence Layer
An optional AI component analyzes job requirements, explains skill gaps, and helps applicants identify relevant evidence. It supports the process without making hiring decisions.



The defining experience
A candidate sees this job:
Junior AI Engineer, Python, SQL, Docker
Instead of merely clicking Apply and uploading a CV, the system shows:
- Python: related evidence attached from their Hangman project.
- SQL: no evidence attached yet.
- Docker: related evidence attached from a containerized application.
- Suggested next step: attach a database project demonstrating SQL.
- Application: choose the relevant projects, write a message and submit.
These are explanatory associations, not verified assessments of the candidate's ability.
The distinction: Traditional job boards organize opportunities around job titles and keywords. PROOFWORK would organize the application experience around requirements and supporting evidence.
We are not claiming no other platform has ever explored this. Our goal is to execute the idea coherently and build something technically substantial.

3. The complete application: pages and features
We're designing the product around two roles: applicants and administrators. Visitors will also be able to browse and apply without creating an account.
We won't introduce separate employer accounts in version 1 because EPITECH only requires an admin-controlled management interface. An employer dashboard can be added later.
A. Public and candidate-facing pages









01. Landing page
The introduction to PROOFWORK. Explain the problem, showcase how evidence-based applications work, and provide clear entry points to explore jobs or create a profile.
Main actions: Explore opportunities, Build your proof portfolio.












02. Opportunity Board
The central page. It features searchable job listings, filters for location, remote work, employment type and skills, plus pagination.
Each job shows its company, title, description preview, location, employment information and two actions: Learn more and Apply.












03. Job details experience
Clicking Learn more reveals a full detail panel alongside the listings or expands an inline section. No popup and no full page reload.
Full description, salary if provided, location, working hours, employment type, company and required skills.












04. Proof Portfolio
Authenticated users add projects and other relevant evidence, such as GitHub repositories, live demos, design work or certifications.
Each proof has a title, description, URL, type and skill tags. Users choose what to share with applications.












05. Application Studio
Applicants fill in their details, write a message and optionally attach relevant proofs. Signed-in users have their personal information prefilled.
The form validates inputs, submits through our API and saves everything in SQL.












06. Candidate Dashboard
Personal account information, saved application history, attached evidence and application status, where the admin has provided an update.
Includes account editing and a way to manage the user's proofs.



We'll also build proper Login, Register and Account Settings pages. A successful login will direct ordinary users to the job board and administrators to the admin interface, as specified in the assignment. jobBoard-project.pdf

4. The core user journeys
Candidate journey
1. Visit the job board and search for an internship
2. Open a listing and read the complete requirements
3. Sign in or continue as a guest
4. Compare requirements to portfolio evidence, if signed in
5. Select relevant proofs and write an application message
6. Submit the application and receive confirmation
7. View application history if signed in

Admin journey
1. Sign in with administrator credentials
2. Create a company and publish a job
3. Define its required and optional skills
4. Receive applications and review attached evidence
5. Update the internal application status and manage listings

These two flows will form the basis of our integration tests and the final demonstration.

5. The intelligence layer: what the AI will actually do
This is where we need discipline.
We should not make AI the foundation of the product. The platform must work when the AI service is disabled.
I propose three AI features, implemented in order.
AI Feature 1: Job requirement extraction
An administrator creates a job advertisement and enters its description.
AI can suggest structured information:
Example input
"We're looking for a backend engineering intern comfortable with Python, REST APIs and SQL. Docker experience would be a plus."

Suggested structured requirements
Skill	Importance
Python	Required
REST APIs	Required
SQL	Required
Docker	Preferred
The administrator reviews and approves the suggestions before publishing.


This is useful because the skills stored in our database become consistent and searchable, instead of existing only as unstructured text.
AI Feature 2: Explain my application readiness
When a user examines an opportunity, the system compares the job's skills with the user's tagged evidence.
We use deterministic database logic for the comparison, not an LLM deciding whether someone is qualified.
The interface can show:
- Required skill with linked evidence
- Required skill without linked evidence
- Preferred skill with linked evidence
- Skills that need the candidate's attention
AI can then explain those differences in natural language using only the job description and selected evidence.
We must be clear that evidence linked to a skill is not the same as verified proficiency. We will not calculate an opaque employability score or automatically reject applicants.
AI Feature 3: Turn missing evidence into a practical project
Suppose a Python developer is interested in a position requiring SQL and Docker, but doesn't have relevant projects to show.
AI could suggest:
Build a containerized REST API with PostgreSQL.
It would explain why that project is relevant, which skills it could demonstrate, and what deliverables the applicant might create.
This is career guidance, not an additional requirement for applying.
What we will deliberately avoid
No AI-generated applicant rankings, no automated rejection, no fabricated job listings, and no claims that AI has verified someone's GitHub code without actually inspecting it.
AI should also receive only the information needed for the requested feature. We won't send application phone numbers, contact details or private messages to an external model by default.
6. SQL database: the complete proposed design
EPITECH requires SQL tables for advertisements, companies, people and applications, with the ability to add relevant tables. Our evidence system needs additional relationships. jobBoard-project.pdf

I propose 11 main tables for version 1.
Table	Main information
companies	ID, name, website, description, location
people	ID, name, email, phone
accounts	Linked person, password hash, role
categories	ID, name
advertisements	Company, contact person, category, title, description, salary, location, hours, type, status
applications	Applicant, advertisement, submitted contact details, message, date, status
skills	ID, normalized skill name
advertisement_skills	Advertisement, skill, required/preferred
proofs	Owner, title, description, URL, evidence type, visibility
proof_skills	Proof, associated skill
application_proofs	Application, selected proof and submission-time snapshot
An account belongs to a person, but not every person needs an account. This allows guest applicants and company contacts to exist in the database without passwords.
How the data connects
Company



Advertisement
Linked to category, skills and contact person

Application
Applicant details, message, timestamp and status

Attached proofs
Selected evidence and preserved submission details

Other key relationships:
- One company can publish multiple advertisements.
- One advertisement can receive many applications.
- One person can submit multiple applications.
- Jobs and skills have a many-to-many relationship.
- Proofs and skills have a many-to-many relationship.
- An application can attach multiple proofs.
We'll implement foreign keys, appropriate unique constraints, indexes and migrations. Important indexes include job status, company, category, application date and the many-to-many relationship keys.
The three bootstrap SQL queries
Before building the complete system, we'll implement the queries from the bootstrap:
1. Count advertisements belonging to each company.
2. Find the advertisement with the most applications.
3. Retrieve all applicants and their messages for a selected advertisement.
This will confirm that our relational model actually works before the frontend depends on it.

What happens when somebody applies?
React application form

POST /api/v1/ads/:id/applications
Validate fields, job ID, account and selected proofs
PostgreSQL transaction
Save applicant/application and selected proof snapshots
HTTP 201 Created
Return application ID and confirmation
Application appears in admin dashboard


Error handling and security
We will implement proper HTTP responses: 200 and 201 for success, 400 for invalid input, 401 for missing authentication, 403 for insufficient permissions, 404 for missing resources, and appropriate conflict/server errors.
Security will include password hashing, server-enforced roles, safe session cookies, CSRF protection where relevant, input validation, parameterized database operations, rate-limited login attempts and ownership checks on portfolio records.

10. What we're building first versus what can wait
This is one of the most important scope decisions.
Priority	Features
P0: Mandatory	SQL, REST CRUD, job board, applications, auth, admin, responsive UI, local tests, README
P1: Product identity	Proof Portfolio, Proof Packs, skill tagging, application evidence, readiness panel
P2: Enhancements	AI extraction, AI explanations, intelligent project suggestions
P3: Future	Employer accounts, GitHub import, optional coding exercises, real job feed integrations, interactive skill maps
One thing I want to emphasize: we are not building coding challenges in version 1.
That adds a complicated challenge-authoring and assessment system, and we haven't demonstrated that applicants or employers actually want it. We can investigate it after the evidence portfolio works.
Likewise, no Three.js universe for the core application. A beautiful, accessible interface with subtle transitions is a better first investment.

13. Critical risks and how we'll handle them
Risk	Our response
Applicants don't have portfolios	Allow conventional applications and multiple kinds of evidence
Skill tags aren't trustworthy	Clearly distinguish linked evidence from verified ability
Employers won't create challenges	Don't require challenges; admin supplies ordinary listings
Job dataset feels fake	Use realistic structured demo data and label it as fictional
Too much complexity	Complete EPITECH requirements and the core flow before adding AI
AI produces inaccurate statements	Ground suggestions in stored job and proof data; require review where needed
Personal information is exposed	Access controls, minimal data sharing and privacy-conscious defaults
Local presentation fails	Seed scripts, reproducible setup and an AI-independent demo
There's also a product risk: employers may still prefer traditional CV screening even when evidence is available.
We shouldn't pretend this is solved. During development, we could show prototypes to a few students and people involved in hiring, then use their feedback to refine the application experience.
